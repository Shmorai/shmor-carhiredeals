How one-way rentals work

Most big brands let you return the car to a different branch, often for a one-way fee (sometimes called a drop fee). The fee depends on the route and the season: it can be small or even free between busy locations in the same country, and much higher for long distances or remote branches.

On the comparison sites, tick “return to a different location” (or similar) and enter your drop-off city to see the full price including the fee.

Crossing borders

Taking a rental car into another country needs the rental company’s permission. Many companies charge a cross-border fee, some restrict certain countries or car types, and international one-way returns (for example Spain to France) can be very expensive because the car has to be brought back. Declare your full route when you book.

Classic one-way road trips

  • Los Angeles to San Francisco along the Pacific Coast Highway.
  • Los Angeles or Phoenix to Las Vegas.
  • Miami to Orlando, or down to the Florida Keys and back.
  • Edinburgh to Inverness via the Highlands.
  • Lisbon to the Algarve.

Frequently asked questions

Why is one-way car hire more expensive?
The rental company has to get the car back to where it’s needed, so it charges a one-way or drop fee. Popular routes between big cities tend to cost less.
