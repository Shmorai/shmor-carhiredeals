Winter tyres and snow chains in a hire car in Europe: what to check

Heading to the Alps or a Christmas market by hire car? Winter tyre rules differ from country to country, and in some places they apply to foreign-registered and rental cars too. Here is what the European Consumer Centre Germany's guide says, and what to ask at the desk.

Do you need winter tyres on a hire car in Europe?

It depends on the country, the date and the road. There is no single European rule. Some countries set a season, some only require winter equipment when conditions are wintry, and some only where road signs say so. In the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, Portugal, Greece and several other countries, winter tyres are not mandatory, according to the European Consumer Centre Germany's guide (last updated 25 September 2026). Always check the latest rules for every country on your route before you collect the car.

Germany and Austria: winter tyre rules for rental cars

Germany has no fixed winter period: tyres must suit the conditions, and winter tyres are required with black ice, packed snow, slush, glaze or slippery frost. The guide gives fines of €60 to €120 and one point for the driver. Since 1 October 2024, tyres marked only M+S are not allowed in winter conditions, though M+S tyres that also carry the Alpine (three-peak snowflake) symbol still are. Where signposted, snow chains are limited to 50 km/h.

Austria has a rule from 1 November to 15 April: winter tyres must be fitted on all wheels when roads are snowy or icy, with a minimum tread of 4 mm for radial tyres. The guide says it is the driver's legal responsibility to carry the required winter equipment, so check the hire car has it, and the rule also applies to foreign-registered vehicles.

France, Italy and Switzerland: signs decide

France requires winter equipment only in signed mountain areas, covering 34 départements, from 1 November to 31 March. Accepted equipment is four winter or all-season tyres with the Alpine symbol, or removable anti-skid devices on at least the two driven wheels. The guide gives a €135 fine, says the vehicle may be immobilised, and states that all vehicles must comply even if registered abroad.

Italy has no nationwide rule: obligations apply where signs require winter tyres or chains on board, usually 15 November to 15 April, with different dates in the Aosta Valley and on the Brenner motorway. Switzerland has no general winter tyre requirement, but signs may require snow chains, and the car must be controllable at all times.

Ask the car hire company before you book

The guide does not say what rental companies must supply, so ask them directly, and get the answers in writing:

  • Will the car be fitted with winter or Alpine-symbol tyres for my dates and route?
  • Are snow chains included, or is there a charge to add them? Can I return unused ones?
  • Can I take the car to the other countries on my route, and does that change the price?
  • Does the insurance or excess cover tyres, wheels and chain damage?

Frequently asked questions

Are winter tyres compulsory on a hire car in Austria?
From 1 November to 15 April, winter tyres must be fitted on all wheels when roads are snowy or icy. The European Consumer Centre Germany says the driver is legally responsible for carrying the required equipment, including in rental cars.
Do I need snow chains in a hire car in France?
Only in signed mountain areas, 1 November to 31 March. You can use four winter or Alpine-symbol tyres, or fit removable anti-skid devices on at least the two driven wheels.
Do I need winter tyres in the UK?
No. The UK is among the countries where winter tyres are not mandatory, according to the European Consumer Centre Germany's guide.
