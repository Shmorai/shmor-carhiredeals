Hiring a car in France: pollution stickers and low-emission zones explained

If your trip takes in Paris, Lyon or Grenoble, a hire car may need a pollution sticker to enter the central zones. The rules catch many visitors out because the sticker is tied to the vehicle, and you may not be the one who arranged it. Here is what GOV.UK says and what to ask before you collect the keys.

Do you need a pollution sticker for a hire car in France?

GOV.UK's travel advice for France says vehicles need a pollution sticker to drive in central Paris, Lyon and Grenoble from 8am to 8pm, Monday to Friday, and that checks can happen at any time. It adds that some older vehicles do not qualify for a sticker at all because of their emissions, and these cannot be driven in central Paris during those hours. GOV.UK also notes that other cities may limit vehicle access to town centres and main roads.

Stickers are applied for through the French Ministry of Environment's official certificat-air website. The official address is linked in our sources.

Who is responsible for the sticker on a rental?

GOV.UK does not say who must arrange the sticker on a rental car, so the answer is in your rental agreement and with the hire company. Before you book, ask:

  • Does the car already carry a valid pollution sticker?
  • If you collect in a city with a low-emission zone, is the car allowed to enter it?
  • Who pays if a fine is issued, and is there an admin fee?

Keep the answers in writing, and check the car when you collect it. If you are comparing deals, you can search from our car hire comparison page or see options from a partner such as VIP Cars, then ask the same questions of whichever company you choose.

Do you even need to drive into the centre?

Often not. Central Paris, Lyon and Grenoble have good public transport, and parking in the middle can be expensive and hard to find. A common plan is to collect the car when you leave the city, or at an airport, such as those on our airport car hire pages, and park outside the zone. If you do need to drive into a low-emission area, check the zone's boundaries and times for your travel dates on the official French sources.

Other documents and rules to check

GOV.UK says you can use a UK photocard driving licence to drive in France, and that hire car companies often have stricter requirements, such as a minimum amount of driving experience, a higher minimum age and an international driving permit. See our guide to the licence check code and IDP. GOV.UK also says the legal alcohol limit is a third lower than in England and Wales, and that in certain mountain regions from 1 November to 31 March vehicles must carry winter tyres or chains: read our winter tyres and snow chains guide for winter trips.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Crit'Air sticker to drive a hire car in Paris?
GOV.UK says vehicles need a pollution sticker to drive in central Paris, Lyon and Grenoble between 8am and 8pm, Monday to Friday. Ask your hire company whether your car has one.
Who pays the fine if a hire car has no sticker?
That depends on your rental agreement. Ask the hire company before you book who pays penalties and whether an admin fee applies, and keep the answer in writing.
Can I get the sticker myself?
Stickers are applied for on the French Ministry of Environment's certificat-air site, as GOV.UK explains. For a rental, check with the hire company first, as the car may already have one.
