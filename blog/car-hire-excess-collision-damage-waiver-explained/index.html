Car hire excess and collision damage waiver: what you owe if the car is damaged or stolen

Third-party insurance is normally included in a hire price, but your own liability for damage or theft to the hire car is a different matter. Which? reported in July 2026 that some "no extra protection" options can leave renters liable for the full value of the car, so it is worth knowing the terms before you collect the keys.

What is the excess on a hire car?

The excess is the amount you pay towards damage or theft before cover applies. Which? says a collision damage waiver (CDW) caps your liability at the excess, "usually around €1,000 to €2,500", rather than the full value of the car. A CDW is not full insurance: you still owe the excess if something happens.

Rental desks sell extra waivers that reduce or remove the excess. Our guide to car hire excess insurance covers the options.

The "no extra protection" trap

In a Which? report dated 31 July 2026, the consumer group described a major rental company adding a "no extra protection" option under which renters who add nothing may be liable for the car's full value if it is stolen or written off. Which? found a car retailing at around £60,000 offered that way at Heathrow. It said the company was not breaking the law, as third-party insurance was included, and the company said liability is displayed clearly. The industry body BVRLA said it was engaging with the company.

The lesson applies to any rental: look for the line that says your maximum financial responsibility, and compare it with what you could afford to pay.

Standalone excess cover: what it does

Standalone excess reimbursement insurance is bought separately, often more cheaply than a desk waiver. Which? points out a catch: standard policies typically cover only the excess of an existing CDW, so they may not pay out on a "no protection" rental. Policies usually work by reimbursement: you pay the rental company first and claim back. Check what is excluded, such as tyres, windscreens or keys, and the claim limits.

Providers selling insurance in the UK should be authorised. You can check any firm on the Financial Services Register, as the FCA advises.

Don't assume your own cover applies

Which? reported that the Association of British Insurers says standard UK motor policies do not typically include cover for rental vehicles, and it found that standard UK credit cards offer no car rental protection, with one premium charge card the exception. Check your policy and card terms; do not assume.

If the car is damaged or stolen

Report it to the rental company straight away and ask for a written record of what happened and what you are being charged. Take photos at the scene and keep receipts for anything you pay. If you hold standalone cover, read its claims process before you travel, because most reimburse you after the rental company has taken the excess, and usually need the paperwork from the rental company to support the claim. Move quickly: policies set time limits for reporting.

Do not sign a damage report you do not understand, and compare it with the photos you took at collection. Disagreements over damage are easier to resolve with dated pictures of the car's condition when you picked it up.

A pre-collection checklist

  1. Find out whether a CDW is included and what the excess is.
  2. Compare the desk's excess waiver with standalone cover bought beforehand.
  3. Ask how much the card hold or deposit will be, as it often relates to the excess.
  4. Photograph and video the car, including wheels and windscreen, and make sure damage is on the form.
  5. Keep paperwork, including the fuel level and return time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CDW and excess?
A collision damage waiver limits what you owe for damage to the car. The excess is the amount you still pay if something happens. Extra waivers or standalone cover can reduce what you pay towards the excess.
Do I have to buy excess insurance for a hire car?
No. It is optional. It is worth considering if you could not comfortably pay the excess.
Does standalone excess cover work with a no-protection rental?
Which? says standard policies typically only cover the excess of an existing CDW, so you should check the policy wording before relying on it.
Does my car insurance cover a hire car?
The Association of British Insurers says standard UK motor policies do not typically include rental vehicles. Check your own policy.
